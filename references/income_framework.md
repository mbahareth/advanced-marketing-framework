# Income-Segmented Persuasion Framework

This document details how to apply the three-tier income-based marketing framework. Different economic segments are driven by fundamentally different psychological needs.

## 1. Low-Income Segment: PROMISES

**Core drivers:** Survival, security, risk aversion, practical utility.  
They fear losing money more than they desire gains.

**Messaging strategy:**
- Strong risk-reversal guarantees ("100% Money-Back", "Cancel Anytime", "Results or Refund").
- Clear, step-by-step tactics.
- Relatable customer testimonials from people exactly like them.
- Immediate, concrete goals.

**Tone:** Reassuring, practical, straightforward, highly specific.

**Example:**
"We promise you will save $50 on your first grocery bill, or we will refund your subscription. Here are the 3 simple steps to start..."

**Common mistakes:** Abstract language, missing guarantees, testimonials from people who feel "rich" or different.

## 2. Middle-Income Segment: ASSETS

**Core drivers:** Aspiration, ROI, efficiency, social mobility, smart investments.

**Messaging strategy:**
- Frame as an asset or investment, never as a cost.
- Show measurable returns (time, money, status).
- Use data, charts, and logical arguments.
- Signal upward mobility and sophistication.

**Tone:** Professional, analytical, aspirational, value-driven.

**Example:**
"Invest in a tool that pays for itself in 30 days. By automating your workflow, you reclaim 10 hours a week — time you can spend scaling your business."

**Common mistakes:** Leading with price, missing ROI framing, pure feature lists without outcome.

## 3. High-Income Segment: PHILOSOPHY

**Core drivers:** Self-actualization, legacy, identity, exclusivity, worldview.

**Messaging strategy:**
- Sell a worldview and identity, not features or ROI.
- Offer unique insights and visionary leadership.
- Position the purchase as an expression of who they are.
- Emphasize exclusivity and opting out of the ordinary.

**Tone:** Visionary, authoritative, minimalist, philosophical, exclusive.

**Example:**
"True luxury isn't about what you own; it's about what you choose to opt out of. We designed this experience for those who value absolute freedom and uncompromising design."

**Common mistakes:** Talking about money saved, heavy feature dumps, mass-market language.

## Summary Table

| Element | Low (Promises) | Middle (Assets) | High (Philosophy) |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Core Triggers** | Guarantees, Testimonials, Tactics, Goals | ROI, Efficiency, Status, Long-term Value | Insight, Ideas, Leadership, Identity |
| **Tone** | Reassuring, practical | Professional, aspirational | Visionary, exclusive |
| **Primary Fear** | Losing money / being scammed | Making a dumb investment | Being ordinary / wasting time |
| **Desired Outcome** | Security + immediate result | Smart growth + status | Alignment with identity & legacy |
